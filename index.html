Sleep, Fatigue, and Athletic Performance

This project examines how sleep exposure relates to explosive athletic performance in trained beach volleyball athletes. Using the study dataset titled Sleep Restriction, Mental Fatigue and Performance, I investigate whether differences in total sleep time are associated with measurable differences in maximum countermovement jump height.

Research question

Core question

How does sleep restriction and sleep-related mental fatigue influence physical and perceptual-cognitive performance in trained athletes? More specifically, this project examines whether total sleep time is associated with maximum countermovement jump height in trained beach volleyball athletes.

My final project focuses on the relationship between sleep and athletic performance because sleep is essential for recovery, neuromuscular readiness, and decision-making under fatigue. In sport, even modest differences in sleep quantity can influence readiness, movement quality, and performance during training and competition. The purpose of this project is to explore whether the sleep variable in the dataset is meaningfully associated with a direct physical-performance measure. This is a useful question because coaches and athletes often want evidence that links recovery habits to performance outcomes that can be measured objectively.

Independent and dependent variables

Independent variable

Total Sleep Time (TST)

The amount of sleep an athlete obtained, measured in minutes and converted to hours for the graph.

Dependent variable

Maximum CMJ height

The maximum countermovement jump height, measured in centimeters, representing explosive lower-body performance.

Why this relationship matters

This project is designed to explore whether sleep exposure is associated with a meaningful athletic-performance outcome. Sleep contributes to recovery, muscle function, and readiness to perform explosive movement patterns. Because countermovement jump performance is a direct measure of power and lower-body explosiveness, it provides a useful benchmark for understanding how sleep may relate to physical readiness. The goal is not to claim causation from one correlation alone, but to investigate whether observed differences in sleep are associated with measurable differences in performance in a realistic athlete sample.

Data source and references

The primary dataset used in this project is the Mendeley Data source titled Sleep Restriction, Mental Fatigue and Performance, which contains research data from the study on mental fatigue and sleep restriction in trained beach volleyball athletes.

  • Barbosa, Bruno. Sleep Restriction, Mental Fatigue and Performance - Dataset. Mendeley Data, v3, 2023. https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/f8r9vhpz59
  • Study description: Mental fatigue and sleep restriction effects on perceptual-cognitive and physical performance in trained beach volleyball athletes.

This analysis uses the actual workbook in the repository, data/Dados para publicação.xlsx, and the relevant variables were identified directly from the source spreadsheet rather than from assumptions or fabricated values.
